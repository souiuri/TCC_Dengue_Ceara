
\vspace{1em}
\begin{center}
    \textbf{ABSTRACT}
\end{center}

\noindent
Dengue represents a major public health challenge in Brazil, highlighting the need to analyze its temporal and spatial distribution. This study aimed to analyze records classified as dengue in the state of Ceará, Brazil, between 2016 and 2025, using computational techniques for data preparation, processing, and visualization. A quantitative, observational, retrospective, and descriptive study was conducted using public data from the Brazilian Ministry of Health and population data from the Brazilian Institute of Geography and Statistics. Data processing was performed in Python, using libraries for data manipulation, graphical visualization, and geographic analysis. The initial filtering process identified 416,095 records associated with Ceará. After applying classification, temporal, and data quality criteria, 215,251 records were included in the temporal analysis and 215,243 in the municipal analysis. The highest annual totals were observed in 2016, with 41,894 records, and in 2022, with 39,940. Approximately 65.5\% of the records were concentrated between March and June. Fortaleza had the highest cumulative municipal total, with 90,035 records. Comparisons between municipal and statewide rates revealed persistent differences throughout the study period, particularly in Brejo Santo, Quixeré, Tabuleiro do Norte, and Fortaleza, whose rates exceeded the statewide rate in eight of the ten years analyzed. Municipal maps revealed variations in the spatial distribution of rates across the study period. The findings indicate that integrating epidemiological and population data with computational techniques enables the characterization of temporal and spatial patterns relevant to public health surveillance. These descriptive results should be interpreted considering limitations in data quality and completeness.
